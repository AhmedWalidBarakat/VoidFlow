# VoidFlow course design

The course is one continuous run of **116 stages, 6 ramps each (696 ramps)**, falling steadily
downward from the spawn to the finish. Each stage is a zone with its own look and a checkpoint at
its start. Ramp shapes are generated by the course builder, but their sizes, gaps and launches are
not random: every gap is checked by the flight solver (the same physics as the player) before it
appears, and a bot surfs the whole course after every change.

This document is the layout section by section, then top-down diagrams.

---

## The rules every section follows

| Rule | How the course does it |
|---|---|
| Momentum carries into the next section | Every ramp ends in a lip aimed at the next ramp's riding line; the gap is solved for the speed you actually have there |
| Catch ramps | Every 4th ramp (5th once it gets hard) is a wide, forgiving "catch" ramp that rebuilds speed and recovers a messy line |
| Fair gaps | The flight solver rejects any gap the player can't make at the designed speed (and rebuilds it shorter); the target is 0 impossible ramps |
| Landing zones | You land onto the face of the next ramp, never onto a point. Easy ramps are wide (up to 13–18 m faces), expert ones narrow (6–7 m) |
| Walls are for surfing | Hall walls stand 95 m clear of every line. Walls near the course are surfable slabs (corners, holes, bowls) or dividing walls with framed windows |
| Recovery | Falling puts you back at the stage's checkpoint with the clock running; **T** restarts the stage |
| Readable geometry | Ramps carry glowing edge trims in the zone's colour; landing lanes are marked with glowing gate caps; the hall floor is a fall |

## Section by section

### 1. Spawn and the start hall (stage 1, ramps 0–1)
- **Purpose:** get your bearings, look at your items, then drop in.
- **Ramp placement:** a sanctum hall with a long runway ending at a drop; the first ramp waits below the edge.
- **Walls:** the hall's walls frame the drop, so there's only one way to go.
- **Expected speed:** standing start, about 400–700 u/s off the drop.
- **Entry / exit:** walk the runway forward; exit is the drop.
- **Landing area:** a very wide first ramp straight below.
- **Difficulty:** none.
- **Skill taught:** pressing into a ramp (let go of W, hold A or D toward it).

### 2. Foundations: Crimson Hall (stage 1, ramps 2–5)
- **Purpose:** the first real surf, slow and wide.
- **Ramps:** classic left-right zig-zag prisms ("blade" and "sweep"), wide faces, small height differences between ramps.
- **Walls:** far away; nothing near the line.
- **Speed:** about 900–1,400 u/s.
- **Entry / exit:** straight ahead, alternating sides.
- **Landing area:** wide faces, gentle transfers.
- **Difficulty:** Easy.
- **Skill taught:** holding a line, ramp-to-ramp transfers, the first air strafes.

### 3. The tier laps (stages 2–29)
Seven laps round four stages each. Every stage in a lap is a new zone:

| Lap stage | Tier | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Intermediate** | Faster transfers, smaller landings. Introduces sweeps, curved rooms and the first **framed windows** (wide, one per stage at most) |
| 2nd | **Advanced** | Speed sections (plunges, drops), big air with **boost rings**, **cross-hall jumps**, the first **slot gates** between pillars |
| 3rd | **Technical** | Everything at once: twin routes, holes in the wall, loops, **helix turns (360°)**, low **beams** to surf under, smaller windows |
| 4th | **Beginner** | A breather: wide catch ramps, gentle curves, plenty of room to recover before the next lap |

- Each lap starts 5% harder than the last (ramps narrower, gaps longer, launches higher).
- Each stage also has a **theme** that sets its ramp mix:
  - **Classic** zig-zag.
  - **Winding** S-bends.
  - **Spiral:** keeps turning one way.
  - **Rollercoaster:** waves, plunges and climbs.
  - **Canyon:** holes and corners.
  - **Big Air:** launch kickers.
  - **Twin Peaks:** twin routes.
  - **Curved Room:** loops and sweeps.
- **Ramps:** a stage's six ramps sit inside one hall. Each flight between ramps passes through a **dividing wall with a framed window**, so every ramp has a chamber of its own and the next ramp only shows as you come through.
- **Walls:** dividing walls give the hall chambers. Corner slabs and hole walls are surfable.
- **Speed:** 1,300–2,600 u/s, rising lap by lap.
- **Entry / exit:** set by the theme. A Spiral turns one way all stage; a Classic stage alternates.
- **Landing areas:** shrink with tier; gates mark a lane on precise landings.
- **Difficulty:** Medium to Hard (Easy on the breather stages).
- **Skills taught, in order of introduction:**
  - Air strafing for distance.
  - Speed control before a gap (air-brake taps).
  - Precision through windows.
  - High-speed launches.
  - Bunny hopping across platforms.
  - Tight corner transfers.
  - Chaining 3–4 ramps in a combo.

### 4. Expert zones (stages 30–39)
- **Purpose:** the hard version of everything from the laps, zone after zone.
- **Ramps:** narrow faces (7–9 m), steep launches, long flights with big height differences, snipes, kickers, zig-zags and stepped dives.
- **Walls:** chambers with **small windows** (11 m wide); slots and beams on the faster flights.
- **Speed:** 2,200–3,000 u/s.
- **Landing areas:** small; gates on most landings.
- **Difficulty:** Hard.
- **Skills:**
  - Precise landings at speed.
  - Mid-air direction changes (flights that shift sideways).
  - Delayed landings on long flights.

### 5. Legend zones (stages 40–74)
- **Purpose:** the hardest generated surfing, after the hardest surf maps.
- **Ramps:** every move at full difficulty, chosen by the zone's theme:
  - Bowls ridden round the rim.
  - 360° helix turns.
  - Plunges into big air.
  - Twin routes.
  - Platforms to bhop across between ramps.
- **Walls:** windows on most flights (every 2nd one), slots and beams. The chambers get tighter.
- **Speed:** 2,500–3,400 u/s.
- **Difficulty:** Expert.
- **Skills:**
  - Very precise routes.
  - Minimal recovery space.
  - Advanced combos.
  - High-speed transfers.

### 6. The real maps (stages 75–115)
- **Purpose:** the run of famous surf maps, ramp by ramp, after the original layouts. Their difficulty tier is shown as players know it (Tier 1–8).
- **Ramps:** laid out by hand in scripts (shape, gap, sideways shift, big air, windows).
- **Walls:** each map's own rooms, with windows where the map has them.
- **Speed:** as the map plays, 2,000–3,300 u/s.
- **Difficulty:** Expert, varying by map.
- **Skill:** reading a real map's flow.

### 7. Final challenge: The Finale (stage 116)
- **Purpose:** "almost impossible, never impossible".
- **Ramps:** the longest gaps the solver allows, the narrowest faces, launches into precise landings.
- **Speed:** up to 3,500 u/s (the speed cap).
- **Difficulty:** the hardest.
- **Exit:** the last flight lands you on the finish plaza.

### 8. Finish
- The clock stops and a results card appears: your time against your best, falls, top speed, stage bests and sum of best.
- Every item in the game unlocks.

## Routes and risk

- **Primary route:** the riding line on every ramp (where the bot surfs).
- **Faster lines** for skilled players:
  - Ride higher on a face and launch faster: windows and slots are sized for every line from the slowest likely speed to 1.8× the design speed.
  - **Twin routes:** a second ramp alongside; pick either.
  - **Big air with boost rings:** fly through the ring for a speed boost; miss it and land slower.
  - **Cross-hall jumps:** a long launch across the hall that skips a turn. Only possible with speed carried in, and solved for that speed.

## Visual language

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Ramp surfaces in the zone's ramp design, with a glowing edge trim | Surf it |
| Dark walls and dividing walls | Walls: go through the framed window |
| Glowing frame round an opening | The way through |
| Glowing gate caps on a landing ramp | Land between them |
| The hall floor, stepping down beneath the ramps in terraces, with a glowing lip on every drop | A fall (back to the checkpoint) |
| Boost rings and Void Shards | Optional speed and loot |

### Inside one stage hall (six ramps, three chambers)

```
   +--------------------------------------------------------------+
   |  checkpoint pad                                              |
   |    P==>  R1 ////////////\                                    |
   |                          \   (flight)                        |
   |=========== dividing wall ===[ window ]========================|
   |                              \                               |
   |      R3 \\\\\\\\\\\\\\  <-----  R2 ////////////\             |
   |       \                                        (catch ramp)  |
   |=====[ window ]===================== dividing wall ============|
   |         \                                                    |
   |          R4 ////////  o boost ring  ->  R5 \\\\\\\\\\        |
   |                     ||  ||  slot gate         \              |
   |                                                 R6 ====> next stage
   +--------------------------------------------------------------+
     floor: terraces stepping down under the ramps, lip lights on every drop

   Legend:  P checkpoint pad   R ramps (/ \ the face you surf)   ==== wall
            [window] framed opening   o boost ring   || || slot gate
```

### Section types (side view of a flight)

```
  window:      ramp ---->   ||[    ]||   ----> ramp         (solid wall, framed hole)
  slot gate:   ramp ---->     |    |     ----> ramp         (two pillars, fly between)
  beam:        ramp ---->   =========    ----> ramp         (beam above: stay low)
  cross-hall:  ramp --launch-->  . . . . long arc . . . . .  ----> far ramp
  helix:       ramp turning 360 degrees as it falls, launching back over where it began
```
